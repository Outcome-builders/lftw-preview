01 / The company

What is Learn Faster to Win?

Learn Faster to Win is the product-management practice of Stefan Wesemann, based at Herengracht 582 in Amsterdam. It works alongside product leaders at established, tech-enabled businesses to turn product investment into business results, faster, and takes responsibility for its part in the result. Learn Faster to Win is the trading name of WMNN Outcomes B.V.; the short form is LFTW and the domain is learnfastertowin.com.

Which legal entity is behind it?

WMNN Outcomes B.V., a Dutch private limited company, registered with the Netherlands Chamber of Commerce (KvK) under number 98627376, at Herengracht 582, 1017 CJ Amsterdam, the Netherlands. Founder and director: Stefan Wesemann. Contact: stefan@learnfastertowin.com.

Whom does it serve?

Ambitious product leaders at established, tech-enabled businesses: companies with customers, a working product or service, and product development as a recurring investment, that own their product decisions, whether development happens in-house or with partners, and where a leader has the authority to act. Outside its focus: businesses still searching for their first product and customers, assignments that mainly fill a temporary product vacancy, engagements without access to the people, information or decisions the outcome needs, and requests to endorse a fixed solution.

What does it do?

It establishes what is holding progress back, agrees what needs to change and helps make it happen, combining training, coaching and hands-on execution. The way of working is the Learn Faster to Win Loop: a direction that guides the work, an engine of four sectors (Investigate, Curate, Validate, Iterate) that turns choices into action and results, a stack that brings what the company already knows into the next piece of work, and compound learning that makes the next decision better.

What are the three ways to work together, and what do they cost?

The LFTW Program, training and coaching on the client's own product challenges, from €15,000 per seat. The LFTW Intensive, a focused engagement with one team on an agreed problem or opportunity, from €35,000 one-off. The LFTW Partnership, work with leadership and several teams on connected obstacles, from €100,000 fixed. Scope, responsibilities, success criteria and price are agreed before work begins.

How does an engagement start?

With the route check on this site: six questions, one lap, and a verdict on which of the three ways fits. If it fits, a thirty-minute call with Stefan Wesemann follows. Before any work starts, the outcome, the responsibilities on both sides and the way success will be judged are agreed in writing.

Where is it based, and how do you reach it?

Herengracht 582, 1017 CJ Amsterdam, the Netherlands. E-mail stefan@learnfastertowin.com. Stefan Wesemann is on LinkedIn; the profile is listed under the person record below.

02 / The person

Who is Stefan Wesemann?

Stefan Wesemann is a product leader and the founder of Learn Faster to Win in Amsterdam. He has more than ten years in product management, from startups to companies with billions in revenue, and ran product at Coolblue, Essent, Postcodeloterij, GrandVision, Vacanceselect, Rebound and WUR. He holds the Professional Scrum Product Owner III certification from Scrum.org, held by a few hundred people worldwide, and trained in Scrum, SAFe and AI programs at MIT and Product Faculty.

Where has he run product?

In-house, at seven companies: Coolblue, Essent, Postcodeloterij, GrandVision, Vacanceselect, Rebound and WUR. Startups to companies with billions in revenue, from the inside, over more than ten years.

Which credentials and training does he hold?

Professional Scrum Product Owner III (PSPO III) from Scrum.org, the highest level of that certification, held by a few hundred people worldwide; the certification is listed on his Scrum.org profile. Training in Scrum and SAFe, and AI programs at MIT and Product Faculty.

What does he stand for?

Judgement first: deciding what is worth doing and judging what came back matters more than producing more. AI is a capability, not a category; the work is still product management. Certainty is not on offer; being as sure as you reasonably can be before you commit is.

Where has he been wrong?

Decentralisation. A set of assumptions with no misses in it is not a set of assumptions, so this record names one. The same rule applies to every bet in the Loop: a verdict is written down, including the ones that went the other way.
