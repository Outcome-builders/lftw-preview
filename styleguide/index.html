Learn Faster To Win helps product leaders at scale-ups build what they should. The company that learns fastest wins, so the brand looks like an instrument: precise readouts, equal segments, hairlines and one accent.

06 / Motion

Readout motion. Values arrive, then settle.

  • Phase bars fill left to right, 240ms, linear.
  • Counters count like round timers, tabular, 600ms at most.
  • Reveals: 200ms opacity plus an 8px rise, sharp ease-out.
  • One purple sweep across the top of the page on load.
  • No bounce, no glow pulses, no parallax, no scroll-jacking. Reduced motion turns everything off.

07 / Do and don't

The metaphor is structural, not a pun.

Do

  • Use round, phase, direction, stack, evidence, change as fixed vocabulary.
  • Show real numbers in changes and ledgers, or show none.
  • Alternate Carbon, Paper and Graphite; neighbours never share a tone.
  • Keep headlines literal: "The fastest learner wins."
  • Photograph Stefan at work, graded to Carbon, purple only in overlays.

Don't

  • No sports imagery and no sports words in copy.
  • No pit wall, timing screen, lap, sector, delta, telemetry or pit stop anywhere.
  • No pole position, full throttle, fast lane, race ahead, chequered flag or podium.
  • No signal colour outside a verdict, change or status.
  • No skew, glow, gradient or motion blur. That was v1.

01 / Purple stays or goes

The case for staying: it is the one accent, it is distinctive in the category, and the system holds with another accent if the colour itself is the problem. Nothing else changes.

02 / How far the vocabulary goes in copy

Proposal: six fixed terms (round, phase, direction, stack, evidence, change) and nothing more. Headlines stay literal.

03 / Barlow Condensed as the face

If the condensed face reads too sporty, the alternative is Archivo Expanded for display: wider, more engineering. Body and evidence stay.
