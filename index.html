01 / The transition

The old game is going away.

Winning the old game is losing. The organisation you have was built to protect production, back when building was the expensive part. Building got cheap, and an organisation that keeps optimising for it gets better and better at the wrong thing.

02 / The cost

The price of the old game.

Staying built to produce gets paid in four places, and every company can put its own number on them. None of the four appear as a cost anywhere. They show up as a roadmap that keeps slipping and a year that is somehow gone. The value of everything below is that number, made smaller.

Cost / 01

The work that shipped and changed nothing.

Failures are cheap and teach you something. This is the feature that landed, worked exactly as specified, and moved nothing anyone can point to.

shipped, moved nothing
Cost / 02

The distance between deciding and knowing.

Every week in there is a week of a team building on an assumption that may already have been wrong when they started.

deciding to knowing
Cost / 03

The answers paid for twice.

Something your company learned two years ago, in a deck nobody can find, researched again from scratch by people who did not know it existed.

paid twice
Cost / 04

What did not happen.

The market not entered, the platform not standardised, the year that went by while the same conversation kept being had. This one costs more than the other three together.

the biggest one
03 / The solution

How to get to the new game.

The new game is played on a playing field that keeps changing. Building keeps getting cheaper, copying keeps getting easier, and the bar is whatever your customer saw somewhere else yesterday. So the right column is not a place you arrive and stay. You get there with a loop, not a plan. The Learn Faster to Win Loop has three parts and one chain running through all of them: assumption, bet, verdict. The pit wall holds what you are betting on. The engine turns an assumption into a bet and a bet into a verdict, one lap at a time. The timing screen holds where you stand, this lap and every one before it.

  1. S1 / InvestigateNowCase

    Read reality.

    Done when you can state the problem, who has it, and what shows it, and none of the three is somebody's opinion.

  2. S2 / CurateNowFastest

    Choose the bets.

    Done when there is a written bet with a metric and a failure point, both set before anything gets built. This is where the lap is won.

  3. S3 / ValidateNowSmallest test

    Build the least to learn the most.

    Done when the question is settled, and a dead bet counts as settled.

  4. S4 / IterateNowVerdict

    Call it, then go again.

    continueacceleratestoppivot

    Done when there is a written verdict, decided on the metric set in advance, and someone who can act on it has read it.

04 / The match

Choosing here is not losing.

Four signs the Loop fits, and four signs it is not a fit. A fast no is a verdict too.

ForFit
  • Tech-enabled product companies. Technology is the engine that runs the business, not the thing being sold. Commerce, logistics, media, financial services, energy.
  • You build with your own people: at least one development team, somewhere below ten.
  • Somebody feels the gap, and somebody can change how decisions get made. Often the same person.
  • Enough decisions to compound. A better way of deciding pays back across many of them.
Not forNo fit
  • Pure technology companies, SaaS and AI-native. You are already organised around product; what you need is not this.
  • Enterprise. The person who can change how decisions get made exists on the chart, not in the week.
  • Companies that do not build. If every decision goes out to an agency, there is no loop to install.
  • The very early. A company still looking for its first customers needs those to land, not a system.
